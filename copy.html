Section 01

Project Overview

India's linguistic diversity extends far beyond the 22 scheduled languages. The Rajasthani language group — comprising Marwari, Mewari, Dhundhari, Hadoti, Mewati, and Bagri — is spoken by an estimated 80–100 million speakers across Rajasthan, Haryana, Gujarat, and Madhya Pradesh, yet remains severely underrepresented in AI language technology.

This project responds directly to the BHASHINI mandate of democratizing language technology for every Indian citizen. We propose a full-stack AI development pipeline — from raw data collection and linguistic validation through to production-grade model training, evaluation, and Bhashini-API integration — executed across six structured stages aligned with the hackathon's official lifecycle.

All models, datasets, and evaluation benchmarks produced will be contributed to the BHASHINI open platform and licensed for public benefit, supporting long-term scalability and community adoption.

Problem Being Solved

Current state-of-the-art models — including Whisper, IndicTrans2, Google Translate, and NLLB-200 — produce unreliable or zero output for these six dialects. A speaker of Hadoti attempting to use voice assistants for healthcare, agriculture, or governance is effectively excluded from the digital ecosystem. This project directly closes that gap.

Section 02

Objectives

OBJ-01

Benchmark existing AI translation models (text-to-text) for Marwari & Mewari ↔ English and other regional languages of India to establish a documented baseline.

OBJ-02

Collect and validate extensive voice and text data for all six dialects (Marwari, Mewari, Dhundhari, Hadoti, Mewati, Bagri) ensuring comprehensive phonemic and syntactic coverage.

OBJ-03

Build and train ASR, TTS, and MT models using newly curated datasets, enabling seamless voice-based communication in and across the six target dialects.

OBJ-04

Implement NLP techniques that preserve linguistic nuances, idiomatic expressions, tone, and grammatical fidelity in all translations and voice interactions.

OBJ-05

Evaluate prototypes using WER, MOS, BLEU/chrF, and use-case coverage to iteratively enhance accuracy — with a specific focus on rural health, governance, and agriculture domains.

OBJ-06

Encourage collaboration among AI researchers, linguists, and community validators; integrate with Bhashini APIs for real-world deployment and ensure DPDP Act 2023 compliance.

Stage 2 — Benchmarking Existing Models
Aug – Sep 2026

Systematically assess current limitations of state-of-the-art multilingual models on all six target dialects. Design a standardized benchmark test suite of 500 sentences per dialect (covering 10 semantic domains), and evaluate against NLLB-200, IndicTrans2, Whisper-large-v3, Google Translate, and IndicWav2Vec.

Deliverables
  • Benchmark test suite: 500 sentences × 6 dialects × 10 domains (curated with native speaker validation)
  • Baseline score report: BLEU, chrF, WER, MOS per model per dialect
  • Failure mode analysis: phonological gaps, OOV rates, code-switching errors
  • Published benchmark leaderboard (publicly shareable)
Stage 3 — Data Collection & Validation
Sep – Oct 2026

Systematically collect and validate high-quality linguistic datasets for all six target dialects. Speech collection via mobile crowdsourcing app; text data from folk literature, regional newspapers, government documents, and parallel corpus construction. All data validated by native speakers through a double-blind annotation process.

Deliverables
  • Speech corpus: 100+ hours per dialect (diverse age, gender, region, accent)
  • Text corpus: 500K+ tokens per dialect in Devanagari
  • Parallel corpus: Dialect ↔ Hindi ↔ English sentence pairs (50K+ per dialect)
  • Idiom & proverb database: 2,000+ entries per dialect with contextual notes
  • Data quality report: SNR analysis, speaker diversity statistics, annotation inter-rater agreement
Stage 4 — Model Training & Optimization
Oct 2026 · Results: 12/10/2026

Fine-tune and train ASR, TTS, and MT models using the curated datasets. Employ parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA/adapter layers) for dialect-conditioned multi-task learning. Cross-dialect transfer learning experiments to determine if Marwari ASR representations benefit Mewari training (and similar pairs).

Deliverables
  • ASR model: IndicWav2Vec-based, fine-tuned per dialect with shared adapter layers
  • TTS model: VITS/FastSpeech2 fine-tuned, targeting MOS ≥ 3.8/5.0
  • MT model: IndicTrans2 fine-tuned on parallel corpus, dialect-conditioned
  • Code-switching handler: Marwari/Mewari–Hindi mixed-language robustness module
  • Model cards with training details, dataset statistics, and bias analysis
Stage 5 — Testing & Iteration
Until 16/10 · Eval: 19–21/10 · Results: 22/10/2026

Validate AI models through structured iterative testing cycles, native speaker user studies, and domain-specific use-case evaluations (healthcare triage, agricultural advisories, governance queries). Use WER, MOS, BLEU, and use-case coverage as primary metrics. Run error-driven ablation studies to isolate sources of failure.

Deliverables
  • Evaluation report: WER / MOS / BLEU / chrF per model per dialect
  • User study results: 20+ native speakers per dialect, structured feedback
  • Iterative improvement log: 3+ training cycles per model with metric deltas
  • Domain coverage analysis: healthcare, agriculture, governance query accuracy
  • Error taxonomy: phonemic, morphological, syntactic, idiomatic failure categories
Stage 6 — Publishing Results & Scalability Planning

Document all findings, open-source datasets and models, integrate with the Bhashini API ecosystem, and prepare a scalability roadmap for production deployment. Section 05

Technical Methodology

Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR)

Backbone: IndicWav2Vec (AI4Bharat) — pre-trained on 40+ Indian language acoustics — will be fine-tuned per dialect using CTC and attention-based decoding. Dialect-conditioned adapter layers (8M parameters) are appended to the shared frozen encoder, enabling parameter-efficient multi-dialect learning. A secondary Whisper-large-v3 fine-tune provides a cross-check baseline.

Data augmentation: Speed perturbation (±10%), SpecAugment (time/frequency masking), room impulse response convolution for acoustic robustness. A custom tokenizer trained on Devanagari sub-word units (SentencePiece, 8K vocabulary) handles morphological richness.

Text-to-Speech (TTS)

Architecture: VITS (Variational Inference with adversarial learning for end-to-end TTS) fine-tuned per dialect speaker cluster. A speaker-conditioning vector (d=256) captures dialect and speaker identity. Target naturalness: MOS ≥ 3.8. Evaluation includes both native-speaker MOS and automated metrics (UTMOS).

Zero-resource extension: For Hadoti and Mewati (lowest data), YourTTS-style cross-lingual transfer from Hindi TTS with dialect-specific prosody adapters will be explored as a data-scarce fallback.

Machine Translation (MT)

Backbone: IndicTrans2 (AI4Bharat) — state-of-the-art for 22 Indian languages — will be fine-tuned on our parallel corpus. Target language pairs: Dialect ↔ Hindi, Dialect ↔ English, and cross-dialect (Marwari ↔ Mewari). An idiom substitution module, backed by our phrase database, intercepts idiomatic expressions pre-translation to prevent literal mistranslation.

Code-switching robustness: A language identification prefix tag (e.g., [MRW], [MEW]) is prepended during training to condition the model on dialect identity and handle mixed Rajasthani–Hindi input naturally.

NLP Preservation Layer

A dedicated post-processing layer handles: (1) honorific and register mapping (Rajasthani has elaborate pronoun hierarchies absent in Hindi), (2) proverb/idiom lookup with contextual substitution, (3) script normalization for dual-script dialects (Mewati: Nastaliq ↔ Devanagari). This layer is applied as a rule-guided post-processor on MT outputs before delivery to end users.

Section 11

Data Security & Compliance

Regulatory Framework

All data collection, storage, and model development activities comply with the Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act 2023 and Bhashini's data governance norms. No personally identifiable information will be retained in published datasets.

Data Collection

Every speaker participant provides written and digital informed consent before recording. Consent forms are available in the speaker's native dialect. Participants may withdraw at any time with full data deletion. Speaker demographic metadata (age band, gender, region) is collected in anonymized, bucketed form only — no names, IDs, or contact details are stored alongside audio files.

Data Storage & Access

Raw recordings are stored in encrypted S3-compatible storage (AES-256 at rest, TLS 1.3 in transit). Access is role-restricted (annotation team, QA team). After corpus finalization, raw audio undergoes automated PII screening before public release. All published data is anonymized at speaker level.

Model Outputs

Trained models are evaluated for demographic bias (age, gender, regional accent parity in WER/MOS scores). Any model exhibiting statistically significant bias (>5% WER disparity across demographic groups) undergoes targeted re-training before release. Model cards include explicit bias analysis sections.

Bhashini API Integration

Production API integration follows Bhashini ULCA data usage norms. No user query data is logged without explicit user consent. Offline/on-device deployment is prioritized for rural users where data sovereignty concerns are heightened.

Expected Outcomes & Impact

Upon successful completion, this project will produce the most comprehensive AI language technology suite ever developed for the Rajasthani dialect group — an estimated 80–100 million speakers who gain meaningful access to voice-first digital services for the first time.

Technical

First unified multi-dialect ASR/TTS/MT system for 6 Rajasthani dialects. Novel dialect-conditioned adapter architecture. Publicly documented cross-dialect transfer learning findings.

Data

600+ hours of dialect speech. 3M+ tokens of validated text. 300K+ parallel sentence pairs. 12,000+ idiom/proverb entries. All openly licensed on Bhashini ULCA.

Research

Research paper submitted to IndicNLP 2027 or ACL 2027. Benchmark leaderboard published. Failure mode analysis informing future low-resource NLP work.

Social Impact

Healthcare triage voice assistants for rural Rajasthan. Agricultural advisory in native dialect. Governance query handling for non-Hindi-literate citizens. Real-world Bhashini integration.

Long-term Vision

The datasets, models, and benchmarks produced here lay the foundation for future work on Rajasthani as a full ISO-recognized language in NLP systems — contributing to India's goal of ensuring no citizen is left behind in the digital transformation, regardless of the language they speak at home.
